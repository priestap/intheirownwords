FROM THE INSTITUTES · III.24.5
❧
“Christ, then, is the mirror in which we ought, and in which, without deception, we may contemplate our election.”

By “mirror,” Calvin means that believers should look to Christ and his promises for assurance of their election, rather than try to uncover God’s hidden decree. His point is pastoral: seek confidence in the Savior to whom the gospel directs you.

JOHN CALVIN

Institutes of the Christian Religion, 3.24.5 ↗

Translated by Henry Beveridge, 1845

OUR APPROACH

A fair hearing.
A faithful reading.

This collection exists to vindicate Calvin and the Reformed tradition where their teaching is misrepresented, by letting their own words be heard.

That purpose calls for care. A statement of belief and a criticism of its logical consequences are different things. Showing that Calvin denied a conclusion does not, by itself, answer a critic’s argument that his theology implies it.

Claim headings are editorial summaries of the tropes in the founding draft, not verbatim quotations attributed to a critic. Responses and context notes are editorial commentary. The quotations are excerpts from the linked texts; readers should consult the surrounding argument.
